To make hot fudge for ice cream, warm cream, butter, brown sugar and golden syrup until smooth, then take the pan off the heat and stir in chopped dark chocolate. For caramel sauce, cook sugar and water until it turns deep amber, then carefully whisk in warm cream and butter. Each takes about 15 minutes and keeps in the fridge for a few days.

We make our own sauces and ripples in our kitchen, and you can do the same at home with a saucepan, a whisk and some patience around hot sugar. Here are our two go-to recipes.

Hot fudge sauce

Hot fudge is a thick chocolate sauce made with cream and butter, and it goes chewy and fudgy as it meets cold ice cream. This makes about 300 ml, enough for six to eight sundaes.

  • 125 ml cream
  • 2 tablespoons golden syrup
  • 40 g brown sugar
  • 25 g butter
  • A good pinch of salt
  • 120 g dark chocolate (around 50 to 70 percent cocoa), finely chopped
  • 1 teaspoon vanilla extract
  1. Warm the base. Put the cream, golden syrup, brown sugar, butter and salt in a small saucepan over medium-low heat. Stir until the sugar dissolves and the butter melts.
  2. Bring to a simmer. Let it bubble gently for two to three minutes, stirring often so it doesn't catch on the bottom.
  3. Add the chocolate. Take the pan off the heat, tip in the chopped chocolate and leave it for a minute. Then stir slowly until the sauce is smooth and glossy.
  4. Finish. Stir in the vanilla. Taste, and add another pinch of salt if it needs lifting.

Serve it warm rather than piping hot, so it doesn't melt the ice cream into soup.

A Hollywood ice cream parlour called C.C. Brown's, which opened in 1929 and served its last sundae in 1996, claimed to have invented the hot fudge sundae.

Caramel sauce

Caramel sauce is sugar cooked until it browns, then loosened with cream and butter. This makes about 350 ml.

  • 200 g caster sugar
  • 60 ml water
  • 150 ml cream, warmed
  • 40 g butter, cubed
  • Half a teaspoon of flaky salt, if you want salted caramel
  1. Dissolve the sugar. Put the sugar and water in a heavy, light-coloured saucepan over medium heat. Stir only until the sugar dissolves, then stop stirring.
  2. Cook to amber. Let it boil, swirling the pan now and then, until it turns the colour of a deep honey or a copper coin. This takes about 8 to 12 minutes. Brush any crystals on the side of the pan down with a wet pastry brush.
  3. Add the cream. Take the pan off the heat. Pour in the warm cream a little at a time, whisking. It will bubble up and steam hard, so stand back and keep your hand out of the way.
  4. Add the butter. Whisk in the butter until smooth, then the salt. If any lumps of hard caramel remain, return the pan to low heat and stir until they melt.

The browning is caramelisation, the heat-driven browning of sugar that gives colour and a buttery, slightly bitter flavour. Table sugar (sucrose) caramelises at around 170°C, which is why a sugar syrup sits pale for ages and then colours quickly. A light pan lets you see the colour change.

Which sauce for which ice cream?

Hot fudge loves vanilla, coffee, mint and peanut butter; caramel loves vanilla, chocolate, banana and anything with crunch. Here is a quick guide.

Hot sugar safety

Caramel is much hotter than boiling water and it sticks to skin, so treat it with respect. A few habits keep the kitchen safe.

  • Keep children and pets out of the kitchen while the sugar is cooking.
  • Use a deep pan: the caramel can rise up when the cream goes in.
  • Warm the cream first. Cold cream makes the caramel spit and seize.
  • Never taste caramel from the spoon or pan. Drip a little onto a cold plate and wait.
  • Turn pan handles inwards, away from the edge of the bench.

If you do get burned, Health New Zealand's burns first aid advice is to cool the burn under cold running water for 20 minutes, and not to use ice, iced water or oils. See a healthcare provider if a child is burned, if blisters form, or if the burn is bigger than the person's palm or on the face or hands. Call 111 for a serious burn.

Storing and reheating

Pour leftover sauce into a clean jar, let it cool, cover it and get it into the fridge within two hours. Love Food Hate Waste NZ, citing Ministry for Primary Industries guidance, says leftovers you plan to reheat can be kept for up to four days in the fridge.

Both sauces set firm when cold. To reheat, stand the jar in a bowl of hot water for ten minutes, or warm the sauce in a small pan over low heat, stirring. In the microwave, use short bursts and stir between each one.

Ice cream itself lives in the freezer, not the fridge. Our guide to storing ice cream at home explains how to stop the tub going icy between sundaes.

Dairy-free and allergy swaps

Both sauces can be made dairy-free, but every ingredient needs checking: the chocolate, the spread and any toppings you add.

  • Dairy-free hot fudge: full-fat coconut cream instead of cream, a dairy-free spread instead of butter, and dark chocolate labelled dairy-free.
  • Dairy-free caramel: coconut cream and a dairy-free spread work well. The sauce is a little looser and tastes of coconut.
  • Nut allergies: both recipes are nut-free as written, but chocolate often carries "may contain" warnings, so read the label.

Serving ideas for summer

Serve the ice cream straight from the freezer and the sauce just warm. A few ideas:

  • The classic sundae: two scoops of vanilla, hot fudge, whipped cream, a few chopped nuts (if everyone can eat them) and a cherry.
  • Caramel affogato: a scoop of ice cream, a shot of espresso and a thin drizzle of caramel. Our affogato guide has the method.
