No-churn ice cream: how to make it without a machine

By The Wooden Spoon team · 15 November 2026 · 7 min read

No-churn ice cream is ice cream you make without a machine: you whip cream until it holds stiff peaks, fold it through a tin of sweetened condensed milk with your flavouring, then freeze it in a covered tin for about six hours. The whipped cream supplies the air a churn would normally beat in, and the condensed milk brings sugar and body that keep it soft. It takes about ten minutes of hands-on time. Here is how it works, the basic recipe, and how to stop it turning icy.

How does no-churn ice cream work?

It swaps the machine for whipped cream. An ice cream maker does two jobs at once: it beats air into the mix and keeps the ice crystals small while it freezes. No-churn ice cream does both jobs before it ever goes in the freezer.

Food writer Jessica Gavin explains that the whipped cream holds air and gives the ice cream a light structure that mimics a churned texture. The sweetened condensed milk adds sweetness and creaminess, and its sugar lowers the freezing point, which helps stop big ice crystals forming. She also notes that condensed milk has had around 60% of its water removed, and less water means less to freeze into crunchy ice.

A summary of the science at Neatorama makes the same point from the other direction: smooth ice cream is all about keeping ice crystals too small to feel on your tongue, and cream and condensed milk both help hold back crystal growth.

The result is not quite the same as churned ice cream. It tends to be richer, sweeter and a little denser, a bit like a frozen mousse. That is not a bad thing on a hot afternoon.

The basic two-ingredient recipe

You need cream, a tin of sweetened condensed milk and a flavouring. That is it. This makes roughly a loaf tin's worth, enough for 8–10 scoops.

You'll need

  • 500 ml cream that whips well (not lite cream; the fat is what holds the air)
  • 1 tin sweetened condensed milk, chilled
  • 2 teaspoons vanilla extract or paste
  • A pinch of salt
  • A loaf tin or shallow container, baking paper, and a hand or stand mixer (a whisk works if you are patient)

Method

  1. Chill everything. Cold cream whips faster and holds more air. Pop the bowl and beaters in the fridge for 15 minutes if you can.
  2. Mix the base. Stir the condensed milk, vanilla and salt together in a large bowl.
  3. Whip the cream to stiff peaks. The peaks should stand up on the whisk without flopping over. Stop before it turns grainy, or you are on your way to butter.
  4. Loosen first. Fold about a third of the cream into the condensed milk to lighten it.
  5. Fold in the rest gently. Use a spatula and a figure-of-eight motion, turning the bowl as you go. Stop as soon as there are no streaks.
  6. Freeze. Spread it into the tin, press baking paper right onto the surface, cover with a lid or foil and freeze for about six hours, or overnight.

Flavour ideas and when to add them

Stir flavourings into the condensed milk before folding, and add chunks and ripples at the very end. Wet or heavy additions go in last so they don't knock the air out.

Go easy on fresh fruit. Raw fruit is mostly water, and water turns to ice. Cook berries down into a thick sauce first, or fold them in just before serving. For fruit served on top instead, see our summer fruit pairing guide.

How to keep no-churn ice cream smooth

Keep the air in, keep the water out, and keep it covered. Most icy batches come down to one of those three.

  • Whip properly. Soft, droopy cream holds less air, so the ice cream sets hard and dense.
  • Fold, don't stir. Every stir knocks out bubbles you worked to get in.
  • Limit watery extras. Juice, fresh fruit and thin sauces all add water.
  • Press baking paper on the surface. Gavin recommends covering the surface to cut down moisture contact, which is also how you avoid freezer burn on bought tubs.
  • Eat it fresh. Home-made ice cream has none of the stabilisers commercial ice cream uses, so Gavin suggests eating it within about two weeks, before ice crystals start to build up.

If it does freeze rock hard, leave it in the fridge for 10–15 minutes before scooping. Our guide to softening ice cream has more ways to get it scoopable without melting the edges.

No-churn vs churned ice cream

No-churn is quicker and needs no gear; churned ice cream is lighter and more flexible. Both are worth knowing.

Allergy and safety notes

The classic no-churn recipe contains milk, and add-ins bring their own allergens. Check every label before you start.

  • Milk: cream and condensed milk are both dairy, so this recipe is not suitable for anyone with a milk allergy. Our post on dairy-free ice cream explains the alternatives, and our oat milk dairy-free range is ready to scoop.
  • Gluten, nuts, soy and eggs: these often hide in biscuits, chocolate, caramel and sprinkles. Our ice cream allergens guide helps you check.
  • Hot sugar: if you make your own caramel or honeycomb for mix-ins, keep kids well back from the pot. Molten sugar burns badly.

Quick no-churn checklist

  • Cream fridge-cold; bowl chilled.
  • Flavourings mixed into the condensed milk first.
  • Cream whipped to stiff peaks, not butter.
  • One third folded in to loosen, then the rest, gently.
  • Chunks and ripples added last.
  • Baking paper pressed on the surface, lid on.
  • About six hours in the freezer; eaten within a couple of weeks.

Frequently asked questions

Can you really make ice cream without a machine?

Yes. No-churn ice cream gets its air from whipped cream instead of a churning paddle, and sweetened condensed milk keeps it soft and sweet. Fold the two together gently, freeze for about six hours, and you get a scoopable ice cream. It is richer and denser than churned ice cream, but very good.

How long does no-churn ice cream take to freeze?

Plan on about six hours, or overnight to be safe. A shallow loaf tin freezes faster than a deep tub. The edges set first, so check the middle with a spoon before serving. If you add a lot of sauce or fruit, it can take a little longer to firm up.

Why is my no-churn ice cream icy?

Usually the cream was under-whipped or knocked flat while folding, so there was too little air, or the mixture had too much watery fruit added. Leaving it uncovered or storing it for weeks also lets ice crystals grow. Press baking paper on the surface and eat it within a couple of weeks.

Can I make no-churn ice cream dairy-free?

The classic version relies on dairy cream and condensed milk, so it is not dairy-free. Dairy-free versions exist but need different ingredients and behave differently, so follow a recipe written for them. If you just want a dairy-free scoop tonight, a ready-made oat milk ice cream is the easy option.
